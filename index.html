What it produces

Not one score. A row per condition and severity, each carrying per-class precision and recall at the threshold you actually deploy, plus the change against the clean baseline.

python -m robustbench run \
  --weights yolov8n.pt \
  --images data/images --labels data/labels \
  --device cuda --out out/

Why a GPU changes the answer

Three things become possible that a laptop cannot do in a working day:

  • Full-set sweeps — 10 conditions × 5 severities × your validation set, not a sample of 30 frames.
  • Adversarial search — PGD and patch optimisation need thousands of backward passes per image.
  • Retraining against the failures — once you know night-at-2 Mbps is where recall dies, you fine-tune on it and re-run the sweep to prove the fix.

The harness already targets --device cuda and falls back to CPU. Nothing else changes.

How the measurement is framed

Not mAP alone

mAP integrates every confidence threshold, including ones you never deploy, and averages classes you may not care about. A model can keep a respectable mAP while nothing fires at the 0.5 cutoff production runs. So the report leads with precision and recall at your threshold, per class, and keeps mAP only as a reference point.

Failure has three shapes

A missed object, a false alarm, and a confidence drift — the third is the quiet one: the boxes are still right, but scores slide under the cutoff and the system stops acting. Tracking the threshold shift per condition is what catches it.

Conditions map to causes

Each corruption is tagged with the deployment cause it represents — lighting, night, optics, speed, sensor, stream bitrate, distance, weather — so a reader sees "night is the problem", not "gamma 3.75 is the problem".
